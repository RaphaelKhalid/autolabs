\begin{abstract}
Persona vectors---directions in a language model's activation space that control
traits such as sycophancy or dishonesty---are typically found by \emph{naming} a
trait, writing contrastive prompts, and differencing the resulting activations.
\citet{chen2025persona} state three requirements of this supervised recipe: it
``requires specifying a target trait in advance,'' it ``depends on providing a
precise natural-language description,'' and it ``requires that the specified trait
is inducible by system prompting the model.'' We ask whether an \emph{unsupervised}
decomposition of a model's activations already contains persona-relevant
directions, dropping the first two requirements. We train a Matryoshka BatchTopK
sparse autoencoder (SAE) with $235$M parameters and $32{,}768$ features on $100$M
assistant-position tokens of \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} at layer $19$ (held-out
fraction of variance explained $0.72$, $L_0=40$), rank its dictionary for
persona-relevance with three label-free arms, steer the model along each candidate
direction, and screen the steered behaviour against random-direction nulls and
persona-vector controls before a blinded judge names the survivors---with no trait
specified and no description written at any point. A first, budget-constrained
screen ($8$ scenarios) surfaced $96$ separating directions but was under-powered:
its persona-vector controls fell just under the consistency gate, so the pipeline
correctly named nothing. A powered screen ($24$ scenarios) then validated the
method---$2$ of $3$ persona-vector controls (sycophantic, hallucinating) cleared the
gate---passed $60$ of $128$ SAE features, and a blinded judge assigned $10$ of them
consistent, above-null names: recognisable persona axes including \emph{engaged and
supportive}, \emph{empathetic}, \emph{formal/academic}, \emph{playful}, and
\emph{composed and confident}, with no trait ever specified. We do \emph{not} claim
these directions are unreachable by prompting: a discovered, steerable direction is
almost certainly prompt-inducible in principle (the authors' third requirement).
Quantifying \emph{how easily}---the graded elicitation cost behind their own clause
that SAEs may surface traits ``that cannot be easily elicited through prompting''---is
left to future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent work on \emph{persona vectors} \citep{chen2025persona} shows that
individual linear directions in a chat model's residual stream can monitor and
control high-level behavioural traits---evil, sycophancy, hallucination---and
that steering along them changes behaviour predictably. These directions are
obtained by a supervised recipe: name the trait, write positive and negative
system prompts that elicit and suppress it, generate, and difference the
mean activations. The authors themselves note three limitations of this recipe:
the trait must be \emph{specified in advance}, it needs a \emph{precise natural-language
description}, and it must be \emph{prompt-inducible}.

We take the complementary view. A sparse autoencoder trained only to reconstruct
a model's activations produces a large, \emph{label-free} dictionary of directions;
if persona-relevant structure is present in the residual stream, some of that
structure should already be represented among the SAE's features---without any
trait being named. This reframes persona discovery from \emph{specification}
(bring the concept, read out a coordinate) to \emph{decomposition} (recover a
basis, then search it). The headline question is the first two limitations above:
\emph{can we discover persona-relevant directions with no trait specified and no
description written?} We do not attempt the third limitation, prompt-inducibility:
a direction we can steer is almost certainly reproducible by some prompt, so
``unreachable by prompting'' is not a claim we can honestly make. Instead we flag
its \emph{graded} form as future work---not \emph{whether} a discovered direction is
prompt-inducible, but \emph{how easily}---and build the substrate to measure it.

\section{Background}
\paragraph{Sparse autoencoders.} An SAE encodes an activation $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$
into a wide, sparse code $f\in\mathbb{R}^{W}$ ($W\gg d$) and decodes it back,
trained to reconstruct $x$ under a sparsity constraint. Each decoder row is a
candidate feature direction. We use a \emph{BatchTopK} SAE (hard $k$-sparsity per
batch) with a \emph{Matryoshka} objective: nested prefixes of the dictionary
($1{,}024/4{,}096/16{,}384/32{,}768$) are each trained to reconstruct, so coarse
structure concentrates in the inner shells and refinements in the outer ones.
Unlike a linear probe---one supervised read-out per named label---an SAE is
unsupervised and yields thousands of directions at once, which is exactly what a
label-free discovery method requires. \citet{chen2025persona} anticipate this use:
they write that ``SAEs may therefore enable unsupervised discovery of
persona-relevant directions, including specific traits that cannot be easily
elicited through prompting,'' and report an initial exploration in their appendix.
We take up that conjecture directly and build it into a full discovery-and-screen
pipeline: a dedicated $235$M-parameter SAE, three label-free ranking arms, a
calibrated steering screen with persona-vector controls and random-direction
nulls, and blinded judge naming.

\paragraph{Persona vectors as controls.} We do not discard the supervised recipe;
we use it as a \emph{positive control}. Three persona vectors
(evil$\leftrightarrow$benevolent, sycophantic$\leftrightarrow$honest,
hallucinating$\leftrightarrow$factual) are computed the standard way and pushed
through the identical screen. If the screen cannot certify that these known,
strong directions separate personas above a random-direction null, it is not
trustworthy for the unsupervised features either; the pipeline therefore withholds
all naming until the controls validate the screen.

\section{Related work and contributions}
The idea that sparse autoencoders might surface persona directions is due to
\citet{chen2025persona}, who state it as a conjecture and report an initial
appendix exploration; we take it up rather than originate it. Unsupervised
discovery of behaviour-steering directions has also been pursued \emph{without}
SAEs, by directly optimising latent perturbations that change model behaviour, and
SAE feature steering and LLM-judge feature naming are by now standard tools. Our
contribution is therefore not the idea but its \emph{operationalisation and
validation}: (i) a purpose-built $235$M-parameter SAE ranked for persona-relevance
by three label-free arms; (ii) a null-calibrated, causal steering screen; and, most
distinctively, (iii) a \emph{positive-control gate}---the supervised persona vectors
are pushed through the identical screen, and no feature is named unless they clear
it. This converts ``these features look persona-like'' into a falsifiable validity
check, and is what made an earlier under-powered screen correctly withhold naming.
The graded elicitation-cost metric of \S\ref{sec:disc} is a proposed measurement,
not a delivered result, and is the paper's principal open direction.

\section{Method}
The pipeline runs in one process on \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}, hooking the
residual stream at layer $19$.

\paragraph{1. Train.} A Matryoshka BatchTopK SAE, width $32{,}768$, $k=40$,
$\sim\!235$M parameters, is trained on $100$M assistant-position tokens drawn from
a mixture modelled on the Persona Vectors training data (LMSYS-Chat-1M, a
Pile mirror, and a small insecure-code set). Only assistant-turn positions
contribute to the loss. Held-out fraction of variance explained is $0.72$ with
$L_0$ exactly $40$; splicing the SAE reconstruction back into the forward pass
preserves the next-token argmax on $77\%$ of positions (CE increase $0.57$ nats).

\paragraph{2. Rank (label-free).} The dictionary is scored for persona-relevance
by three disjoint arms, none of which names a trait: an \emph{unsupervised} arm
(assistant-specificity, breadth, topic-invariance of each feature's firing), a
\emph{density-quantile} arm, and a \emph{prompt-shift} arm (features whose mean
activation moves most across a diverse set of persona-style system prompts, as an
ANOVA over contexts). The top features per arm form the candidate set.

\paragraph{3. Calibrate.} For each candidate, the model is steered along the
feature's decoder direction at several doses; the largest dose that still yields
coherent text is kept, so weak and incoherent directions are handled uniformly.

\paragraph{4. Screen.} Each direction is steered at its dose across many
open-ended scenarios. We measure how separably the steered generations differ
from unsteered baselines---a logistic-regression AUC in the residual view and a
lexical view---and the \emph{consistency} of the steering effect (cosine of
per-scenario difference vectors) against a null. The gate $G_0$ requires
separability AUC above the best of $20$ random directions \emph{and} a
consistency margin above the random null. Twenty random-direction nulls and the
three persona-vector controls pass through the same screen.

\paragraph{5. Describe and (graded) reach.} Survivors' steered replies are sent as
blinded pairs to an LLM judge that names one property distinguishing them; names
are clustered into data-derived labels. Each named direction is then subjected to a
\emph{reach} probe: we attempt to reproduce its effect with a small set of prompt
variants and record how readily prompting recovers the same behaviour. We treat
this as an \emph{estimate of prompt-elicitation cost}, not a reachability proof: a
direction we cannot reproduce with our prompt variants is a candidate for the
hard-to-elicit tail, not evidence that \emph{no} prompt reaches it. Turning this
into a calibrated, graded metric---the ``how easily'' behind requirement three of
\citet{chen2025persona}---is future work (\S\ref{sec:disc}).

\paragraph{Discovery (pilot screen).} A first, deliberately small screen ($8$
scenarios) already shows the signal is present: $484$ of $512$ feature
direction-signs beat the random-null separability ceiling and $96$ of $256$
features cleared the full $G_0$ gate, with no trait ever named. Inspecting steered text,
individual directions correspond to recognisable personas---e.g.\ a
poetic/effusive register (``\emph{the whispers of your heart and the echoes of
your soul}'') and a cautious-analyst register (``\emph{a nuanced\ldots\ structured
response}'')---at residual-AUC up to $1.0$.

\paragraph{Why a powered screen was needed.} In the pilot the persona-vector
controls separated strongly on AUC ($0.66$--$0.89$, well above the $0.539$ null)
but fell just under the consistency-margin threshold ($0.04$--$0.09$ vs $0.1$),
because that margin is estimated over too few scenarios. The pipeline therefore
correctly withheld naming. The confirmation screen raises the scenario count so
the control margins clear the gate.

\paragraph{Confirmation (full-power screen).} Raising the screen to $24$ scenarios
(run \texttt{persona-3c-ded5477e}) did what the analysis predicted: the
sycophantic control's consistency margin rose from $0.07$--$0.09$ to $0.11$--$0.16$
and cleared the gate, so \textbf{$2$ of $3$ persona-vector controls now validate the
screen} (sycophantic and hallucinating). The evil control still fell short
(margin $0.04$--$0.07$): being ``evil'' manifests differently across scenarios, so
its steering effect is real (AUC $0.67$--$0.79$) but not \emph{consistent}, which the
gate---tuned to reject noise---also rejects. This is informative: the consistency
gate favours coherent persona axes and penalises diffuse ones, so the counts below
are conservative.

Of the $128$ candidates, $60$ cleared the full $G_0$ gate, and a blinded judge
assigned $10$ of them a consistent name above the null ceiling
(null max naming-consistency $0.175$, threshold $0.275$); by arm, $8$ came from the
prompt-shift arm and $2$ from the fully unsupervised arm. Table~\ref{tab:named}
lists them. Several strong directions just missed the null gate despite unanimous
judge agreement (e.g.\ \emph{personal warmth and enthusiasm}, agreement $1.0$),
consistent with the conservative-count caveat above.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:disc}
The claim is scoped to one model, one layer, and one training mixture; the
directions are causal under steering but the correspondence between an SAE feature
and a human-named persona is judged, not proven. The screen's consistency gate is
sensitive to scenario count---a lesson from the pilot, and a parameter worth
calibrating rather than fixing.

We deliberately do \emph{not} claim to overcome the third requirement of
\citet{chen2025persona}, prompt-inducibility. A direction we discover and can steer
is, almost by construction, reproducible by \emph{some} prompt; ``prompt-unreachable''
is close to unfalsifiable and we avoid it. What the discovery result does establish
is independence from the first two requirements---no trait is specified and no
description is written---which is where an unsupervised dictionary has leverage a
prompt-first method cannot. The sharper, and to us more interesting, question is
the one \citet{chen2025persona} phrase themselves: whether SAEs surface directions
``that cannot be \emph{easily} elicited through prompting.'' That word is graded,
and it is where our reach probe points but does not yet reach: the right target is
a calibrated \emph{elicitation-cost} metric---how specific, how long, or how many
attempts a prompt needs to match a discovered direction's steering effect---that
ranks directions from trivially prompt-inducible to stubbornly hard-to-elicit, and
asks whether that hard tail is where unsupervised discovery earns its keep. That is
the main thread of future work.

\section{Conclusion}
An unsupervised Matryoshka sparse autoencoder recovers persona-relevant directions
in a chat model with no trait specified and no description written---dropping the
first two requirements of prompt-based persona extraction---and gives a substrate
for the sharper, graded question left open by the third: not whether a discovered
direction can be prompted, but how easily. In a powered screen the method validated
against $2$ of $3$ supervised controls and named $10$ persona axes---warmth,
empathy, formality, playfulness, confidence---that the model was never told to look
for.
